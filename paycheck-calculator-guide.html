How to Calculate Your Take-Home Pay in 2026

Understand gross pay, net pay, paycheck taxes, and pay frequency before planning your budget.

Your salary or hourly rate tells only part of the story. The amount deposited into your bank account is usually lower because payroll may subtract taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, and other deductions. This guide explains the basics of estimating take-home pay and how to use a paycheck calculator as a planning aid. You can start with annual salary, monthly salary, or hourly pay, then choose how often you are paid.

1. Gross pay vs. net pay

Gross pay is what you earn before taxes and deductions. Net pay, or take-home pay, is what remains after applicable withholding and deductions. Two people with the same gross salary may receive different net pay because of filing status, location, benefits, and withholding choices.

2. Common pay frequencies

  • Weekly: usually 52 checks a year.
  • Biweekly: usually 26 checks a year, paid every two weeks.
  • Semimonthly: usually 24 checks a year, often twice a month.
  • Monthly: usually 12 checks a year.

For example, a $65,000 salary equals about $2,500 gross per biweekly paycheck ($65,000 divided by 26), or about $2,708.33 semimonthly ($65,000 divided by 24). These are before taxes and deductions.

3. Enter annual salary, monthly salary, or hourly pay

If you know your annual salary, enter the yearly gross amount. If you are paid a monthly salary, choose Monthly salary and enter the gross amount for one month; the calculator converts it to an annual estimate by multiplying by 12. For example, $5,000 per month equals $60,000 gross per year. Do not enter a monthly amount in the annual salary field, or the estimate will be incorrect.

If you are paid by the hour, use the hourly option and enter your hourly rate and usual hours worked per week.

4. Convert hourly pay to annual income

For a simple estimate, multiply hourly pay by hours worked each week and then by 52. For example, $25 per hour × 40 hours × 52 weeks equals $52,000 gross annual pay. This assumes the same hours each week and excludes unpaid leave, overtime, and variable schedules.

5. Taxes and deductions that may affect pay

US employees may have federal income tax withholding, Social Security and Medicare taxes, state or local income taxes, health insurance premiums, retirement contributions, and other deductions. Not every item applies to every worker, and some pre-tax deductions can change the income used for particular tax calculations.

  • Federal income tax: depends on taxable income, filing status, deductions, credits, and withholding elections.
  • Social Security and Medicare: payroll taxes that generally apply to covered wages, subject to applicable rules and limits.
  • State and local taxes: vary by where you live and work.
  • Benefits and retirement: depend on your employer and your choices.

6. How to use a paycheck calculator

  1. Choose annual salary, monthly salary, or hourly pay, then enter your gross earnings in the matching format.
  2. Select the pay frequency shown on your payslip or offer.
  3. Choose filing status where requested.
  4. Enter deductions if you know the amount per paycheck.
  5. Review the breakdown and compare it with an actual payslip when available.

Our US Paycheck Calculator uses simplified federal tax assumptions and illustrative state tax percentages. It does not use official state-by-state tax tables, so use it for broad planning, not an exact payroll prediction.

7. Improve your estimate

Check current IRS guidance and your state tax agency. Confirm your pay schedule. Multiple jobs, bonuses, commissions, overtime, dependents, local taxes, and complex benefits may not be captured by a simple calculator. Ask payroll or a qualified tax professional about individual questions.

8. Budget using net pay

Build your monthly budget around the money you actually receive, not just the headline salary. List essential costs first, then savings goals and flexible spending. If your pay varies, use a conservative estimate and review your budget against actual deposits.

Common paycheck planning questions

How do I calculate take-home pay?

Estimate applicable taxes and payroll deductions, then subtract them from gross pay. A calculator can help with planning, but actual withholding and benefits determine the final amount.

Is biweekly the same as semimonthly?

No. Biweekly usually means 26 paychecks yearly; semimonthly usually means 24.

Why is my paycheck lower than annual salary divided by 12?

Annual salary is gross pay. Taxes, payroll taxes, insurance, retirement contributions, and other deductions can reduce deposited pay.

Can a calculator tell me my exact paycheck?

Not always. Actual payroll depends on current rules, location, withholding elections, benefits, and personal details. Verify important figures with official sources or payroll.
