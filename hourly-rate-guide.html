How to Calculate Your Freelance Hourly Rate

Updated September 2026 · 6 min read

Ask ten freelancers what they charge per hour and you will get ten different numbers — most of them guesses. The truth is that your rate should not be a feeling. It is a calculation, and once you see the formula, you will never undercharge again.

Why Guessing Your Rate Always Ends Badly

Employees have a hidden advantage: their employer pays for taxes, software, holidays, and sick days. Freelancers pay for all of that themselves — but out of the same hourly number. If you take a salary you liked, divide it by 2,000 hours, and charge that, you will lose money every single month.

The Real Formula

Your hourly rate has to cover four things at once:

  1. Your target income — what you want to actually keep per year.
  2. Your business expenses — software, equipment, internet, courses, insurance.
  3. Your taxes — self-employed people typically owe 25-40% of income in tax.
  4. Your unbillable time — and this is the big one.

Here is the surprise that changes everything: only 50-70% of your working hours can ever be billed to a client. The rest goes to emailing, invoicing, finding clients, learning, and admin. If you work 40 hours a week, you can realistically bill about 24.

So the formula becomes:

Hourly rate = (target income + expenses) ÷ (1 - tax rate) ÷ (working weeks × hours per week × billable %)

A Worked Example

Say you want to keep $50,000 a year. You spend $5,000 on business expenses, expect a 25% tax rate, take 4 weeks off, work 40 hours a week, and 60% of your time is billable:

  • Billable hours: 48 weeks × 40 hours × 60% = 1,152 hours
  • Gross needed: ($50,000 + $5,000) ÷ 0.75 = $73,333
  • Minimum rate: $73,333 ÷ 1,152 = $63.65/hour

Charging $40 "because it feels right" would have cost you $27,000 a year.

Add a Margin on Top

The number you calculated is your floor — the rate below which you lose money. Add 10-25% on top for profit, negotiation room, and the simple fact that experienced freelancers deliver faster and better. Your floor is $63? Quote $75.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I charge hourly or per project?

Hourly protects you when scope is unclear; per project rewards your speed as you get better. Many freelancers quote projects using their hourly rate as the internal calculation — see our quote guide.

What if my calculated rate feels too high for my market?

Raise your billable efficiency first (better clients, less admin), then nudge rates up with each new client. Never stay below your floor for long — that is a slow business death.

How often should I raise my rates?

Every 6-12 months, or with every 2-3 new clients. Existing clients can keep old rates for a set period if you tell them new rates apply to new work.

Do experienced freelancers really bill fewer hours?

No — they bill the same or more, but their output is worth more, which is exactly why the best freelancers move from hourly pricing to value-based project pricing over time.
