How to Calculate a Freelance Hourly Rate

A sustainable freelance rate should cover the income you want to earn, the costs of running your business, the hours you can realistically bill to clients, and a reasonable allowance for taxes. This hourly rate calculator is designed for freelancers, consultants, designers, developers, writers, virtual assistants and other independent professionals around the world.

Choose a currency from the list and enter all amounts in that same currency. The currency selector changes how the result is displayed; it does not convert money between currencies or adjust the calculation for local cost of living.

How the hourly rate calculator works

The calculator uses your target personal income, annual business expenses, weeks off, working hours per week, estimated effective tax percentage and billable-time percentage. It first estimates the annual revenue needed, then divides that amount by the number of hours you expect to invoice.

Planning formula: Annual revenue target = (target income + annual business expenses) ÷ (1 − estimated tax rate). Estimated minimum hourly rate = annual revenue target ÷ annual billable hours.

Annual billable hours are estimated as (52 − weeks off) × working hours per week × billable-time percentage. This is a simplified planning model, not a full accounting or tax calculation.

What counts as a business expense?

Include reasonable annual costs that help you operate your freelance business. Depending on your work, these might include software subscriptions, computer equipment, internet and phone costs, professional insurance, payment processing fees, training, workspace costs and accounting services. Use amounts that fit your own circumstances and local accounting rules.

Why billable hours matter

Not every hour you work can be invoiced. Finding clients, preparing proposals, answering emails, marketing, bookkeeping, learning new skills and managing projects all take time. If you work 40 hours a week but can invoice only 60% of those hours, the calculator should use 60% as your billable-time estimate rather than assuming all 40 hours are paid by clients.

Worked example using any currency

Suppose your target personal income is 50,000 units of your chosen currency, annual business expenses are 5,000, your estimated tax provision is 25%, you take four weeks off, work 40 hours per week and bill 60% of your working time. These figures are illustrative only and are not tax guidance for a specific country.

  • Available working weeks: 52 − 4 = 48.
  • Estimated annual billable hours: 48 × 40 × 60% = 1,152.
  • Estimated annual revenue target: (50,000 + 5,000) ÷ 0.75 = 73,333.33 currency units.
  • Estimated minimum hourly rate: 73,333.33 ÷ 1,152 = approximately 63.66 currency units per hour.

This result is a starting point. Compare it with the value you provide, the complexity of the work, client budgets and relevant market rates. You may choose to quote above your minimum to allow for profit, uncertainty, negotiation or future investment.

How to choose a realistic tax assumption

Tax rules differ by country and may depend on business structure, residency, income level, deductions, social contributions and other factors. The percentage entered in this calculator is your own estimate; the tool does not determine your actual tax liability. If you are unsure, consult official tax guidance for your jurisdiction or a qualified local tax professional.

Common hourly pricing mistakes

  • Dividing an income goal by every hour in a year instead of realistic billable hours.
  • Forgetting recurring costs, equipment replacement, professional services or payment fees.
  • Using a tax percentage that does not reflect your own situation.
  • Copying another freelancer’s rate without considering experience, scope, risk or client market.
  • Never reviewing the rate as costs, demand, skills and workload change.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this calculator outside the United States?

Yes. Select a listed currency and enter your own income and expense amounts in that currency. The formula is a general planning estimate; it does not apply country-specific tax rules or convert currencies.

Does the calculator convert currencies?

No. It formats the result in your selected currency, but all inputs must already be in that same currency. Exchange rates and currency conversion are not included.

What is a good billable-time percentage?

It depends on your work and business stage. Client-facing delivery may take a larger share of time for some freelancers, while new businesses may spend more time on sales and administration. Track your hours and use a realistic estimate rather than assuming every hour is billable.

Does the result include my actual taxes?

No. The tax percentage is an assumption you provide. The result is not a tax return, tax assessment or personalized financial advice.

Should I charge exactly the minimum rate?

Not necessarily. Treat the result as a planning floor, then consider your experience, service value, project risk, market conditions, profit goals and time spent on non-billable work before setting your client-facing rate.

Important: This calculator provides an estimate for planning and educational purposes only. It does not replace accounting, legal, tax or financial advice. Check the rules and obligations that apply where you live and operate.
