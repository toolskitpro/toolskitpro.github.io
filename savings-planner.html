How to use a freelance savings planner

Freelance income can change from month to month, so a useful plan separates money by purpose instead of treating every client payment as spendable income. Enter your average monthly income, regular living and business expenses, a tax-reserve percentage that fits your situation, and a savings target.

What the estimates mean

  • Income after tax reserve: your income after setting aside the percentage you entered.
  • Leftover after expenses: the estimated amount remaining after your stated expenses.
  • Monthly savings: the percentage of after-tax income you selected.
  • Emergency fund target: six months of the expenses you entered; this is a planning benchmark, not a rule for every household.

Make the plan work with irregular payments

Use a conservative income estimate rather than your best month. When a larger payment arrives, first reserve the appropriate amount for tax and upcoming bills. Keep a separate buffer for months with fewer projects, and review the plan when your expenses or income pattern changes.

Frequently asked questions

Does this planner calculate my actual taxes?

No. The tax percentage is a budgeting assumption you choose. Actual tax depends on your country, income, business structure and personal circumstances.

How much should a freelancer save each month?

There is no universal percentage. Choose an amount that fits your after-tax income, essential costs, debt obligations and goals, then adjust as your situation changes.

Is six months of expenses always enough?

Six months is a common planning target, not a guarantee. Your ideal buffer may be higher or lower depending on income volatility, dependants, insurance and access to other funds.

Does this tool store the numbers I enter?

The calculation runs in your browser and this page does not send the entered figures to a GenFreeInvoice server.
